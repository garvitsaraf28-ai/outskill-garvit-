# Interview Prep — Top 25 Questions & Suggested Answers (Sales → Programs)

Answers are frameworks in your own voice — adapt, don't memorise. Lead with a result, then the how.

**1. Why move from Sales to Programs?**
Because the work I'm proudest of over three months has been Programs work in all but title — building a learning platform, researching curriculum, writing program documentation. Sales taught me what the program promises the learner; Programs is where I want to make sure we deliver it.

**2. Walk me through the AI onboarding platform.**
New reps were trained by pulling seniors, managers, and management into repeated manual mock-calls — costly and inconsistent. I built an 11-level platform: company foundations → product → sales process → live AI-scored mock-calls against escalating personas (peer to director) → live-work reporting, with manager dashboards. It's live in Hyderabad and approved for company-wide rollout.

**3. You have no Programs background — how do you know it's good pedagogy?**
I designed it the way a good program works: clear progression, knowledge checks before practice, realistic simulation, and feedback loops. I went through the Accelerator as a learner, so I built from what actually helped me learn — and I treat it as iterative, improving it from real usage, not assuming it's finished.

**4. How did you decide the curriculum/levels?**
Backward from "deal-ready": I listed everything a rep must know and do, grouped it into a logical sequence (know the company → know the product → know the process → practise → perform), and gated each level so nobody practises before they've learned. Difficulty escalates persona by persona so confidence builds.

**5. How did you research the Programs source-of-truth deck?**
I compiled it from across the business — every program, its pricing journey, and how learners move through the funnel across Sales, Ops, Support, Mentors, CS, and Marketing — and structured it so any team could read it once and understand the whole ecosystem. It's now the cross-functional onboarding reference.

**6. Programs is very different from Sales — what will be hardest?**
The formal craft — instructional-design frameworks and learner metrics like completion and NPS. I've done the instincts version; I want to learn the discipline version. I've already scoped a short course and I learn fastest by building, which I've shown.

**7. Tell me about taking initiative no one asked for.**
The platform. The brief was "find a problem and build a project." I could have done something small; instead I took on the training bottleneck end-to-end, solo, over ~15–20 days — and it went from a project to something the company wants to adopt.

**8. How do you handle ambiguity / no clear instructions?**
I define the problem sharply first, then build the smallest useful version and improve it with feedback. With the platform I spent 6+ hours up front getting the context and data exactly right before building — clarity at the start saved rework later.

**9. How did you use AI to build it, and what was hard?**
I used Claude to build it, but the hard part was mine: feeding it accurate, structured company knowledge — scripts, pricing, payment routes, personas — and iterating until the mock-calls and scoring felt real. AI accelerated execution; the judgement about what to build was the work.

**10. Give an example of data driving a decision.**
I analysed Dial Nexa dialer data into per-rep and team views — leads, workshop show-ups, conversions, revenue — so the Associate Director, Growth could see exactly where to coach and reallocate, instead of guessing from gut feel.

**11. How do you measure whether onboarding/a program is working?**
Leading indicators (completion, knowledge-check and mock-call scores, readiness) and lagging ones (time-to-first-sale, ramp speed). The platform already captures the leading set per trainee; the next step is tying it to ramp outcomes.

**12. Tell me about working with senior stakeholders cross-functionally.**
I built analytics for the Associate Director, Growth, decks used by HR in inductions, and coordinated with the Hyderabad launch. I learned to give leaders the decision, not the raw data, and to make what I build easy for others to actually use.

**13. How did you balance sales targets while building all this?**
I protected selling hours and built in the margins — early mornings, evenings, weekends. Hitting ~129% of target in my first full month while shipping the platform is the proof I can carry delivery and a build at once.

**14. What did going through the Accelerator as a learner teach you?**
Where attention drops, which moments land, and what learners actually need versus what we assume. That learner's-eye view is exactly what I'd bring to designing and improving programs.

**15. How would you improve one of our current programs?**
I'd start by instrumenting the learner journey — where do people drop, disengage, or get confused — then fix the highest-impact gap first. I'd rather change one thing that moves completion than redesign everything on instinct.

**16. Describe a time you failed or made mistakes while building.**
Early versions of the platform had scoring and flow I got wrong; I made plenty of mistakes over those weeks. I treated each as a signal, reworked it, and the friction is why the final product holds up. I'd rather ship, learn, and fix than wait for perfect.

**17. How do you prioritise when everything feels important?**
Impact over effort. I ask what unblocks the most people or protects revenue, and I do that first — that's why I automated reporting and built the training tool rather than polishing smaller things.

**18. How do you document processes so others can follow?**
I write for the person who knows nothing yet — plain language, logical order, one source of truth. The Programs deck and the five induction decks were built exactly that way, which is why other teams could pick them up and use them.

**19. First 30/60/90 days in Programs?**
30: learn how each program is designed, delivered, and measured today, and where the pain is. 60: own a defined piece — documentation, a workflow, or a delivery improvement — and ship it. 90: propose and start one improvement backed by learner data.

**20. How do you handle feedback/criticism?**
I want it early and specific — it's cheaper than shipping the wrong thing. Every improvement in the platform came from someone pointing out a flaw. I separate my ego from the work.

**21. Tell me about influencing people to adopt something you built.**
The platform is being adopted because it visibly saves senior time and shows managers real trainee progress — I let the value do the persuading, made it easy to start, and supported the Hyderabad team as they came on. Adoption follows usefulness, not pressure.

**22. How do you scale a program from one team/office to many?**
Standardise the core, document it so it runs without you, and make the data visible so managers can self-serve. That's the pattern behind the platform going from my team to Hyderabad to a company-wide rollout.

**23. Why you over an external hire with Programs experience?**
I already know the company, the programs, and the learner — from selling them and completing the Accelerator myself — and I've shipped a working learning product here. An external hire starts at context zero; I start delivering on day one.

**24. What are you weak at / need to learn for Programs?**
Formal instructional-design methods and learner-experience metrics. I'm honest that I've done the practical version and want the disciplined version — and I ramp fast on anything I can build with.

**25. Where do you see yourself in 2–3 years?**
Owning program design and delivery end-to-end — running programs that measurably change outcomes for learners, and building the systems that let them scale. This move is the first step toward that.
