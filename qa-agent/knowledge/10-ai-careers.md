# AI careers, transitions, and income paths

## The "AI Generalist" role

Companies increasingly need people who can *apply* AI inside a function — automate
workflows, deploy agents, judge outputs — without being ML engineers. That's the AI
Generalist: your existing domain expertise (HR, finance, marketing, ops…) plus practical AI
skills. It's a career *upgrade* path, not a career *change*: you become the AI-capable
version of what you already are.

## Realistic income paths (no guarantees, ever)

- **Levered employment:** become the person on your team who automates and leads AI
  adoption. Raises and role expansions follow visibility, not certificates — which is why
  the program ends in shipped, demonstrable work.
- **Side income / freelancing:** automating for small businesses is a genuinely large,
  underserved market. Typical starting services: lead-management automations, content
  pipelines, support bots, report automation. Growth is effort-dependent; the program's
  Monetization Mastery 101 and Kairos Fellowship cover client acquisition.
- **Consulting / service line:** consultants and agency owners add "AI-powered" delivery,
  raise rates, and productize knowledge into reusable workflows and agents.
- **Career transition:** moving *toward* AI-heavy roles (automation specialist, AI ops,
  prompt/workflow designer) — a portfolio of working builds is the entry ticket.

Compliance note: Outskill never promises a job or a specific salary. When learners ask
"how much will I earn?", the honest answer is a range of observed paths plus "it depends on
your effort and market" — e.g. outcomes framed as what learners have done, not what's
guaranteed.

## Skill gap: what to actually learn (priority order)

1. **Prompting well** — the multiplier on everything else.
2. **One automation tool deeply** (n8n) — where visible business value lives.
3. **Agent thinking** — decomposing work so AI can do it.
4. **Judgment** — evaluating outputs, knowing failure modes, data privacy basics.
5. **Your domain, restated as AI use-cases** — the part only you can bring.

## How fast can a beginner get useful?

Days to be personally productive (drafting, summarizing, analysis in your role); ~2 weeks of
guided building to ship a first real automation or agent (that is literally the Accelerator's
14-day design); months of practice to be confidently client-ready. Consistency beats
intensity.

## For leaders (VPs, CXOs, founders)

The leader's job is strategy, governance, and adoption — which tools are allowed, what data
can be shared, which processes get automated first, how the team gets trained. Learning
hands-on first ("you first, then your managers") is the pattern that works; employer-funded
enrollment via L&D budgets and corporate invoices is common at this level.
